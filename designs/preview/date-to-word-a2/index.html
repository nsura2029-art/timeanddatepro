How to write a date in formal words

Written dates are required for legal documents, financial instruments, and official forms in most jurisdictions. Unlike numeric dates, words cannot be altered after signing — making them the standard for contracts, cheques, affidavits, and government records.

The legal date format

The legal format — "Eleventh day of July, Two Thousand Twenty-Six" — follows the centuries-old convention of expressing the day as an ordinal, the month by name, and the year in full words. This format eliminates ambiguity from numeric date systems and provides a permanent record that resists tampering.

How to write a cheque date

On a cheque, the date line traditionally uses the banking format: "Eleventh July, Two Thousand Twenty-Six". The day is written as an ordinal, the month by name, and the year spelled out in full. Many banks reject cheques with abbreviated years.

FAQ

How do I write a cheque date?
Use the banking format: "Eleventh July, Two Thousand Twenty-Six".
What's the legal format?
"Eleventh day of July, Two Thousand Twenty-Six" with the "day of" connector.
Does it support years before 1000?
Yes. Year 1 = "One", year 9999 = "Nine Thousand Nine Hundred Ninety-Nine".
Can I use it for non-English dates?
Currently English (US/UK). French, German, Spanish, Japanese coming in Phase 2.
